Access has two layers. The node decides **who reaches an app at all**, from the
app's access rule and the system state, on every request. Everything finer —
who may edit which record — is the app's own code, which can read the caller's
standing with `env::caller_org()`.

Every rule on this page is enforced by the system group's state machine when
the command applies ([The system group](/protocol/system-group/)); the API
checks most of them first so a refused request records nothing.

## App access

An app instance carries one `Access` rule (`types::Access`), set at creation
and changed by its owner (`POST /apps/{app}/access`):

| Rule | Who reaches the app |
| --- | --- |
| `owner` (default outside an organization) | The owner only |
| `accounts { accounts }` | The owner and these accounts |
| `org` (default inside an organization) | Every member of the app's organization, any role |
| `team { team }` | The team's members (direct rows, and inheritance into an open team) |
| `public` | Anyone, signed in or not (as `Caller::Public` when not) |

The owner always reaches their app, except that an organization's app is
reached only by the organization's current members: its owner and the accounts
an `accounts` rule lists stop reaching it, and receiving its events, once they
leave or are removed (the rule keeps their names, so being added back to the
organization restores it). A caller who does not reach it gets `404`,
so its existence is not disclosed; with no credential, `401`. The check reads
fresh system state, so removing someone from an organization or team holds on
every node before their next request ([Reads of the system
state](/protocol/system-group/#reading-the-system-state)).

A member whose **direct row** in the app's team is **read-only** still reaches
the app and its calls run, but a call that would write anything is refused with
`403`. A blob upload by them is refused
before it starts.

## Organizations

`POST /orgs { name }` creates an organization with its creator as **owner**.
Members have one role each, ordered `guest < member < admin < owner`:

| Role | May |
| --- | --- |
| `guest` | Reach the organization's `org`-access apps |
| `member` | Also own apps in the organization and create top-level teams if they hold `CREATE_TEAM` |
| `admin` | Manage members and their roles (below owner), capabilities, teams, organization metadata; delete the organization's apps; approve releases, upgrade apps and approve outbound hosts (they hold every capability) |
| `owner` | Everything, plus ownership and deleting the organization. Release approvals are also open to `APPROVE_RELEASES` holders, upgrades and outbound approvals to `MANAGE_APPLICATION` holders (see Capabilities) |

There is exactly one owner. `SetOrgRole { role: owner }` by the owner moves
ownership and makes the previous owner an admin; the owner cannot be removed,
only replaced. Leaving is removing oneself. Removing a member clears their
capabilities, metadata and every team row they had. `DELETE /orgs/{org}`
(owner, with a passkey step-up) deletes the organization with its teams,
invites and apps.

An organization also keeps metadata for its apps and frontends to read (admins,
or members holding `MANAGE_METADATA`), per-member metadata (the member or an
admin), the mero-tee releases its `APPROVE_RELEASES` holders approved, the
outbound prefixes its `MANAGE_APPLICATION` holders approved, and its recent
upgrade runs. An organization may also be a **publisher**: a package signing key is
bound to it by the key's own signature (`APPROVE_RELEASES` holders send the
binding), and it then approves releases, and (where the package says so) runs
upgrades, for every instance of that key's packages
([Publishers](/protocol/governance/#publishers)). Each metadata value is at most 16 KiB of JSON. Any
member reads, with `GET /orgs/{org}/apps`, the apps they reach or manage
there with the release each runs and its last upgrade (who, of which kind),
the releases that govern each app, and the releases the cluster's nodes run
([Reading the approvals](/protocol/governance/#reading-the-approvals)).

## Teams

Teams (`types::Team`; `meroctl`'s *groups*) nest (`parent`), move
(`UpdateTeam { parent }`, never under themselves), and are one of two kinds:

- **Open** (`join: everyone`): whoever reaches the parent team — at the top,
  the organization — is a member too, unless they were denied
  `JOIN_OPEN_TEAMS` there. Membership flows down an unbroken chain of open
  teams.
- **Restricted** (`join: invite`): only direct rows. A restricted team is a
  wall: nothing flows through it.

A **direct row** has a role (`lead`, `member` or `read-only`) and capabilities.
A team's creator leads it. Whoever leads a team, or an ancestor team, or is an
organization admin, **manages** it: renames it, adds and removes members,
creates teams under it, moves it within what they manage, and deletes it.

- Removing someone from a team, or their leaving it, also removes their direct
  rows in every team below it, so a restricted child team (a private channel
  under an app's team) does not keep them in.
- A member who leaves a team, or whom a manager removes, does not come back by
  inheritance (`Team::removed`), so
  leaving an open team sticks. They return only when a manager adds them again
  or they redeem an invite that names the team; they cannot rejoin by
  themselves.
- A member may join an open team they already reach as a plain member row, or
  leave any team themselves.
- Deleting a team deletes the teams below it and the apps deployed to them.
  When there are apps, it takes the deleter's passkey: `DELETE
  /orgs/{org}/teams/{team}` answers `412 CONFIRM_REQUIRED` with the `apps` that
  go in its details, and the consent (`__delete_team { team, apps }`) names
  exactly those, so an app added since is not deleted on an old approval.
- Reads are filtered: a team someone neither reaches nor manages is left out of
  `GET /orgs/{org}` and answers `404` on its own route.
- A team keeps metadata the app defines (a folder's name and colour), set by
  those holding `MANAGE_METADATA` for it.

An app deployed with `access: team` must be created by someone who writes that
team (a member whose row is not read-only).

## Capabilities

Beyond their role, a member holds capability bits (`types::caps`, the
`MemberCapabilities` values): organization-wide, with a default the
organization sets, and per direct team row (a new row takes the member's
organization value). Admins and team leads hold them all where they manage.

| Bit | Name | Checked by the node |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `1 << 0` | `CREATE_APP` | Creating an app in the organization (on its team, or org-wide) |
| `1 << 1` | `INVITE_MEMBERS` | Minting an invite (org-wide, or on each team it names) |
| `1 << 2` | `JOIN_OPEN_TEAMS` | Inheritance into open teams (granted by default) |
| `1 << 3` | `MANAGE_MEMBERS` | Adding non-lead rows and setting capabilities in a team |
| `1 << 4` | `MANAGE_APPLICATION` | Upgrading the organization's apps (on the app's team, or org-wide), organization-wide upgrade runs, and approving the organization's outbound hosts (org-wide only) |
| `1 << 5` | `CREATE_TEAM` | Creating a top-level team (or moving one to the top) without being an admin |
| `1 << 6` | `DELETE_TEAM` | Deleting a team one does not manage |
| `1 << 7` | `MANAGE_VISIBILITY` | Switching a team between open and restricted |
| `1 << 8` | `MANAGE_METADATA` | Organization and team metadata |
| `1 << 10` | `APPROVE_RELEASES` | Approving (or withdrawing) the mero-tee releases that hold the apps the organization governs: those of the packages it publishes, and its own apps of packages whose key is not bound; and binding a package signing key to the organization (org-wide only: a team grant does not reach it) ([Release approvals](/protocol/governance/#release-approvals)) |

Bit 9 is unused: v1's `MemberCapabilities` spends it on `CAN_AUTHOR_ON_BEHALF`,
so a client that sends v1's `ALL` grants nothing new by accident. A grant is
`POST /orgs/{org}/capabilities { account, capabilities }` by an admin, with
their passkey (as every membership change: roles, removals and leaving, team
rows; `412 CONFIRM_REQUIRED` first); `GET
/orgs/{org}` lists who may approve releases (`releaseApprovers`).

## Invites

`POST /orgs/{org}/invites { role, teams?, ttlSecs?, uses? }` returns a token
`inv_…`, after the inviter approves it with a passkey (`412 CONFIRM_REQUIRED`
first; the consent binds `{ inviteKey, role, teams, expiresAt, uses }`). Only
the token's Ed25519 public key is stored (`inviteKey`, the key whose seed is
`SHA-256("calimero.invite.v1\0" ‖ token)`). An invite expires (7 days by default) and
admits `uses` accounts (1 by default). Its role may not exceed the inviter's and
is never `owner`. An inviter below admin needs `INVITE_MEMBERS` on each team the
invite names (organization-wide when it names none), and may name only teams
they see (a member of, or a manager): an organization-wide grant does not open a
restricted team they are not in.
`POST /invites/{token}/redeem`, by a signed-in person (no passkey prompt): the
node signs the redemption with the token's key, which every node checks
where it applies, so a node that saw only the log cannot redeem an invite. It adds them at the invite's
role (or keeps a higher role they already have) and gives them a plain row in
each named team. Only a redemption that does one of those spends a use: an
account already in the organization at the invite's role or above, and in every
team it names (an owner opening their own link, say), gets the organization back
and leaves the use for the person the invite was made for. The last use removes
the invite; redeeming it after that, or after its organization is deleted, is
refused `400 REJECTED` ("this invite was used up, revoked or never existed"), and
past its expiry "this invite has expired". A redemption an earlier release logged
replays as that release applied it (it spent a use whatever it changed): the
API marks new ones (`RedeemInvite { free_when_unchanged: true }`). In the same
way, a `CreateInvite`, `RemoveTeamMember` or `DeleteTeam` an earlier release
logged replays without the rules above (the teams the inviter sees, the rows
below, the passkey for apps); the API marks new ones (`teams_seen`, `subtree`,
`apps_need_consent`).

## Recovery

An organization may hold a **recovery key** (`Org::recovery`): an Ed25519 key
whose 32-byte seed its owner keeps offline; the organization stores only the
public half (`publicKey`, base64url). It is set at creation (`POST /orgs
{ name, recoveryKey }`) or by the owner later, with a passkey (`POST
/orgs/{org}/recovery { publicKey }`, consent `org:<org> / __set_recovery_key /
{ publicKey }`; `null` removes it). An organization created before recovery
keys has none until its owner sets one.

Whoever holds the seed hands the organization to the account they are signed
in as: the client signs `"calimero.org-recovery.v1\0" ‖ org ‖ 0 ‖ account ‖ 0 ‖
nonce` with the seed and sends only the signature (`POST
/orgs/{org}/recovery/start { nonce, signature }`). Every node checks it
against the organization's current key where `StartRecovery` applies and
spends it, so a node that saw the log cannot redirect or replay it. The
recovery then waits `RECOVERY_DELAY_SECS` (72 hours, judged at the commands'
own times); members see it as `recovery.pending` on `GET /orgs/{org}`. The
owner may cancel it with a passkey (`__cancel_recovery { account }`), and
setting a new key cancels it too. Once the delay has passed, the account it
names finishes it with its own passkey (`__finish_recovery { org }`): it
becomes the owner, every previous owner leaves the organization and its teams,
and the apps they owned in it become the new owner's. One recovery runs at a
time (`409 RECOVERY_PENDING`). Recovery never changes an account's passkeys:
the previous owner's account and its other organizations are as they were.
