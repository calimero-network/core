Access has two layers. The node decides **who reaches an app at all**, from the
app's access rule and the system state, on every request. Everything finer —
who may edit which record — is the app's own code, which can read the caller's
standing with `env::caller_org()`.

Every rule on this page is enforced by the system group's state machine when
the command applies ([The system group](/protocol/system-group/)); the API
checks most of them first so a refused request records nothing.

## App access

An app instance carries one `Access` rule (`types::Access`), set at creation
and changed with `POST /apps/{app}/access` by its owner, an organization admin,
or whoever manages its team's apps (`MANAGE_APPLICATION` on its team, which a
team's leads hold) and, when it moves to another team, that team's apps too;
any access but `team` needs `MANAGE_APPLICATION` organization-wide:

| Rule | Who reaches the app |
| --- | --- |
| `owner` (default outside an organization) | The owner only |
| `accounts { accounts }` | The owner and these accounts; for an instance of a package with an app team in its organization, only those of them in that app team |
| `org` (default inside an organization) | Every member of the app's organization; a guest only while the organization has no app team |
| `team { team }` | The team's members (direct rows, and inheritance into an open team), its owner included: an owner not in the team does not reach it |
| `public` | Anyone, signed in or not (as `Caller::Public` when not) |

The owner always reaches their app, except that an organization's app is
reached only by the organization's current members: its owner and the accounts
an `accounts` rule lists stop reaching it, and receiving its events, once they
leave or are removed (the rule keeps their names, so being added back to the
organization restores it). An app on a team is reached by its owner only while
they are in that team, as `owner_in_org` asks of the organization: an owner who
leaves the team, is removed from it, or loses it when an open team is closed
without them, stops reaching it (a person removed from Chat's app team no
longer reaches the channels they created), and has it back once they are in
the team again. Likewise an `accounts` instance (a DM, a private channel) of a
package that has an app team in its organization is reached only by those of
its owner and list who are in that app team (`SystemState::app_team_of`):
someone removed from Chat's team, who leaves it, or who loses it when it
closes, is out of Chat's DMs too, and has them back once in the team again. An
instance of a package with no app team there keeps its list alone. `ScopeApps`
entries an earlier release logged (without `owner_in_team` or
`accounts_in_app_team`) find who reaches an instance as that release did. A
caller who does not reach it gets `404`,
so its existence is not disclosed; with no credential, `401`. The check reads
fresh system state, so removing someone from an organization or team holds on
every node before their next request ([Reads of the system
state](/protocol/system-group/#reading-the-system-state)).

An organization's app is **managed** by its owner only while they are a member
of the organization (`member` or above, as owning an app there takes;
`SystemState::owner_acts`). An owner who leaves, is removed or is made a guest
keeps their name on the record but none of its powers: changing its access
(so they cannot make it `public` to reach it again), its metadata, transferring
or deleting it, upgrading it or making an upgrade key for it, uploading files
to it or deleting its blobs. The organization's admins (and its owner) still
transfer it to a member or delete it, so an organization cleans up an app whose
owner left; `POST /apps/{app}/transfer` is open to them too, with their own
passkey. A personal app (no organization) stays its owner's alone. Likewise, an app on
a team is managed by its owner only while they are in that team: out of it,
they keep their name on it and none of the powers above, and the
organization's admins (or whoever manages the team's apps) transfer it. The
rules are judged where each command applies; `SetAccess`, `SetAppMetadata`,
`TransferApp`, `DeleteApp`, `UpgradeApp` and `CreateApiKey` entries an earlier
release logged replay as that release judged them (ownership alone, and no
admin transfer, without `owner_in_org`; the owner without the team, without
`owner_in_team`), as do `ScopeApps` entries, which find who reaches an
instance; the API marks new ones.

A member whose **direct row** in the app's team is **read-only** still reaches
the app, but only its views run for them: any other call is refused with `403`
before it runs. A blob upload by them is refused
before it starts.

## Organizations

`POST /orgs { name }` creates an organization with its creator as **owner**.
Members have one role each, ordered `guest < member < admin < owner`:

| Role | May |
| --- | --- |
| `guest` | Once the organization has app teams, reach only the apps of the teams they have a direct row in (no `org`-access app, no open top-level team by inheritance); before that, its `org`-access apps as before. Always see only the people of their teams |
| `member` | Also own apps in the organization and create top-level teams if they hold `CREATE_TEAM` |
| `admin` | Manage members and their roles (below owner), capabilities, teams, organization metadata; delete the organization's apps; approve releases, upgrade apps and approve outbound hosts (they hold every capability) |
| `owner` | Everything, plus ownership and deleting the organization. Release approvals are also open to `APPROVE_RELEASES` holders, upgrades and outbound approvals to `MANAGE_APPLICATION` holders (see Capabilities) |

There is exactly one owner. `SetOrgRole { role: owner }` by the owner moves
ownership and makes the previous owner an admin; the owner cannot be removed,
only replaced. Leaving is removing oneself. Removing a member clears their
capabilities, metadata and every team row they had. `DELETE /orgs/{org}`
(owner, with a passkey step-up) deletes the organization with its teams,
invites and apps.

An organization also keeps metadata for its apps and frontends to read (admins,
or members holding `MANAGE_METADATA`), per-member metadata (the member or an
admin), the mero-tee releases its `APPROVE_RELEASES` holders approved, the
outbound prefixes its `MANAGE_APPLICATION` holders approved, and its recent
upgrade runs. An organization may also be a **publisher**: a package signing key is
bound to it by the key's own signature (`APPROVE_RELEASES` holders send the
binding), and it then approves releases, and (where the package says so) runs
upgrades, for every instance of that key's packages
([Publishers](/protocol/governance/#publishers)). Each metadata value is at most 16 KiB of JSON. Any
member reads, with `GET /orgs/{org}/apps`, the apps they reach or manage
there with the release each runs and its last upgrade (who, of which kind),
the releases that govern each app, and the releases the cluster's nodes run
([Reading the approvals](/protocol/governance/#reading-the-approvals)).

### Members' security

The owner and admins read what tells them a member may be locked out, with
`GET /orgs/{org}/members?detail=security` (their own session on the
cluster's origin; never an API key or a package-bound session). For each
member: the self-set `name` (`memberMetadata.name`), `role`, the `teams` they
are in, `passkeys` (`1`, `2` or `"3+"`: whether they have a backup, not how
many devices they own), `recoveryCode` (set or not), `lastActiveDay` (the UTC
day of their latest sign-in or session exchange; `null` when the node knows
neither) and `sessions` (how many live sessions they hold).

| Reader | Sees | Never |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The account holder | Everything about their own account: passkeys with labels and times, sessions, activity (`/auth/me`, `/auth/sessions`, `/auth/activity`) | - |
| The organization's owner and admins, about its members | The fields above | Credential ids, passkey labels (names people choose for their devices), sign counts and times, sessions, origins, activity, a recovery under way |
| A member below admin | Nothing new (`403`) | as above |
| A deployment's managers, about customer organizations | `memberCount` and `activeMembers30d` per organization (`GET /deployments/{id}/orgs`) | Any account, name or per-member fact |
| The operator | Nothing new: the Ops API has no read path into accounts | Anything per account |

A removed member leaves the listing at once, and someone outside the
organization is answered as if it did not exist (`404`).

## Teams

Teams (`types::Team`; `meroctl`'s *groups*) nest (`parent`), move
(`UpdateTeam { parent }`, never under themselves), and are one of two kinds:

- **Open** (`join: everyone`): whoever reaches the parent team — at the top,
  the organization — is a member too, unless they were denied
  `JOIN_OPEN_TEAMS` there. Membership flows down an unbroken chain of open
  teams.
- **Restricted** (`join: invite`): only direct rows. A restricted team is a
  wall: nothing flows through it.

A **direct row** has a role (`lead`, `member` or `read-only`) and capabilities.
A team's creator leads it. Whoever leads a team, or an ancestor team, or is an
organization admin, **manages** it: renames it, adds and removes members,
creates teams under it, moves it within what they manage, and deletes it. A
member who writes a team (a row that is not read-only, or inheritance into an
open team) and holds `CREATE_CHILD_TEAM` there also creates a **restricted**
team below it, and leads that one: this is how any member of an app's team
makes a private channel. An app team holds `CREATE_CHILD_TEAM` in its default
capabilities, so it is on unless an admin turns it off; a `CreateTeam` entry an
earlier release logged replays with only the parent's managers creating below
it (`member_child_teams`).

- Removing someone from a team, or their leaving it, also removes their direct
  rows in every team below it, so a restricted child team (a private channel
  under an app's team) does not keep them in. Closing an open team (`POST
  /orgs/{org}/teams/{team} { join: invite }`) does the same for whoever loses
  it then, being in it by inheritance alone: their rows below it go, and they
  are not barred, so opening it again lets them back into the team (not into
  the channels). An `UpdateTeam` an earlier release logged replays keeping
  those rows (`close_drops_below`).
- A member who leaves a team, or whom a manager removes, does not come back by
  inheritance (`Team::removed`), so
  leaving an open team sticks. They return only when a manager adds them again
  or they redeem an invite that names the team; they cannot rejoin by
  themselves.
- A member may join an open team they already reach as a plain member row, or
  leave any team themselves.
- Deleting a team deletes the teams below it and the apps deployed to them.
  When there are apps, it takes the deleter's passkey: `DELETE
  /orgs/{org}/teams/{team}` answers `412 CONFIRM_REQUIRED` with the `apps` that
  go in its details, and the consent (`__delete_team { team, apps }`) names
  exactly those, so an app added since is not deleted on an old approval.
- Reads are filtered: a team someone neither reaches nor manages is left out of
  `GET /orgs/{org}` and answers `404` on its own route.
- A team keeps metadata the app defines (a folder's name and colour), set by
  those holding `MANAGE_METADATA` for it.
- A team may hold **default capabilities** (`Team::default_capabilities`):
  everyone in the team holds them there on top of their organization-wide
  ones, unless their row sets its own. Set with `POST /orgs/{org}/capabilities
  { team, capabilities }` (no `account`) by whoever manages the team or holds
  `MANAGE_MEMBERS` there, with a passkey (`__set_default_capabilities { team,
  capabilities }`). This is how "members may invite others to this app" is
  switched on per app.

An app deployed with `access: team` must be created by someone who writes that
team (a member whose row is not read-only).

## App teams

An **app team** is a top-level team whose metadata marks a package:
`metadata.app = { package, name }` (`Team::app_package`). An organization has
at most one per package: marking a second is refused. Only an owner or admin
makes, changes or drops the mark: installing an app in an organization is
theirs. The app's instances use `access: team` on its app team, or on a
restricted team below it (a private channel). The organization's **mode** is
derived from its app teams, never stored, and answered as `mode` on
`GET /orgs/{org}` and `GET /orgs/{org}/app-teams`:

| Mode | When | Means |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `legacy` | No app team | Apps use `org` access, as before app teams. Behaves as standalone. |
| `standalone` | Every app team open | Everyone in the organization has every app; members may invite to the app (the team's default `INVITE_MEMBERS`) |
| `shared` | Some app team restricted | Each app lists its people; invites name the app team; only leads and admins invite unless an admin turns on the team's default |

`POST /orgs/{org}/app-teams { apps?: [{ package, name?, instances?, join?, keep? }], shared? }`
(an owner or admin, with a passkey: `__scope_apps { apps, shared }`,
`SystemCmd::ScopeApps`) gives apps their own access lists in one log entry.
For each package (absent: every package with an `org`-access instance in the
organization) it finds or makes the app team (its creator leads a new one),
gives a plain row to every member who reaches the instances now, and moves the
instances (absent: every `org`-access instance of the package) to `access:
team` on it. `shared` (absent: whether the organization then holds two app
teams or more) closes every app team, everyone it held kept as a direct row,
and takes `INVITE_MEMBERS` out of its default. Nobody loses an app. A new
app team's default holds `CREATE_CHILD_TEAM` in either mode (`INVITE_MEMBERS`
too while standalone), so its members make private channels below it; app
teams made before keep the default they had until an admin sets it.

To keep one app apart and leave the others as they are, a package names its
own `join` in the same request: `invite` closes that app team alone, in the
same log entry that fills its rows, and `everyone` opens it again (`shared`,
absent, then changes no other team; `shared: true` with an `everyone` is
refused). A close keeps, by default, everyone who reaches the app then, each
as a direct row. With `keep: [account, ...]` (members of the organization
only) the team's leads and those accounts keep it, each with a row, and
everyone else in it, by a row or by inheritance, loses it, together with their
direct rows in the teams below the app team (its private channels), as a
removal from the team takes them; they are not barred, so opening the team
again lets them back in. Each package's answer names its team's `join` after the step and
how many people it `dropped`. A single team also opens or closes with `POST
/orgs/{org}/teams/{team} { join }` (`MANAGE_VISIBILITY`, an admin's anywhere),
which fills no rows: closing that way, only the existing direct rows stay, and
whoever loses the team loses their rows in the teams below it too.
`GET /orgs/{org}/app-teams` lists, to any member, each app team's package, id,
name, `join`, member count, default capabilities and whether the caller is in
it or manages it (a guest: only their own).

### Every app added later

In an organization that keeps its apps apart, a new app's team holds only the
people named when it is made. A member may instead hold a standing grant,
**every app added later** (`Org::all_apps`, `allApps` on `GET /orgs/{org}`):
each app team made from then on gives them a plain row, whoever else it
names, so they reach every new app, open or closed. That is an app team made
by `ScopeApps` (closed with a `keep` list too: they are kept), one a move
makes in the organization ([Moving an app](#moving-an-app)), and a team an
admin marks as an app team (unless they were removed from that team). It
gives nothing in an app team already there: an admin adds them to those as
anyone else.

The grant is the owner's or an admin's to give, with a passkey: on an invite,
`POST /orgs/{org}/invites { ..., allApps: true }` (the consent's arguments
then name `allApps: true`; `403` below admin), which gives it to whoever
redeems it, and later, `POST /orgs/{org}/all-apps { account, allApps }`
(`__set_all_apps { account, allApps }`, `SystemCmd::SetAllApps`; `false`
stops it, the rows already made stay). A member redeeming an `allApps`
invite gets the grant, which spends a use. It ends with their membership:
leaving, removal, or a recovery that removes them. `ScopeApps`, `MoveApp` and
`UpdateTeam` carry `all_apps_rows`, which the API always sets; entries an
earlier release logged replay making no such rows.

While an organization has app teams, creating the first instance of a package
that has none there is an admin's. In a
`shared` organization a member sees, in `GET /orgs/{org}`, only the people of
the teams they are in and its owner and admins; a guest sees only their teams'
people in every mode; owners and admins see everyone. Calimero Home, the
portal that manages people, invites and apps across an organization, is one
first-party package every cluster installs; it uses these routes and needs
nothing else from the node.

## Moving an app

`POST /apps/{app}/move { toOrg, bringMembers? }` (`SystemCmd::MoveApp`, with a
passkey: `<app> / __move { toOrg, bringMembers }`) moves an app instance to
another organization in one log entry. Its data stays where it is: the same
group, replicas and state. Calimero Home's "Merge organizations" moves every
app of one organization into another this way.

- **Who**: whoever manages the app where it is now (its owner while a member,
  or an admin there; a personal app's owner), as an admin or the owner of the
  target. Judged on the state of the moment, so a member removed from the
  source, or an admin of the target demoted, is refused.
- **Payer**: its file bytes, compute runs and quotas go to the target. A move
  past the target's file or compute quota is refused (`429 QUOTA_EXCEEDED`),
  as is one to a target that approves none of the releases holding the app now.
- **Access**: `org` access, or `team` access on its package's app team,
  becomes `team` access on the target's app team of the package. The target's
  is used, or made when missing: open, unless every app team of the target
  is restricted (`shared: true`), where it is restricted like its others; an
  app team an admin closed alone leaves it open. The mover leads a new one,
  whose default capabilities are any new app team's (`CREATE_CHILD_TEAM`, and
  `INVITE_MEMBERS` while open). A `legacy` target, and a raw upload (no
  package), get `org` access instead, so moving an app in never changes the
  target's mode. `owner`, `accounts` and `public`
  access stay as they are, though only the target's members reach a
  non-public app from then on. An app on any other team (a restricted team
  below the app team, a plain team) is refused: that team's leads, read-only
  rows and capabilities are grants of the source, which the target's admins
  never saw. Give it `org` or app-team access first.
- **Owner**: stays, and must be a member of the target once the move is done,
  or the move is refused: an organization's app is owned by one of its members,
  and only a transfer the owner or an admin consents to changes who that is.
  An owner who reaches the app now gets a row on the target's app team, as an
  owner reaches and manages an app on a team only while in it.
- **`bringMembers`**: every member of the source who reaches the app joins the
  target at their role there, capped at `member` (a guest stays a guest; an
  admin comes in as a member, and the target's admins grant more themselves),
  and gets a row on the target's app team. Someone already in the target keeps
  their role. A personal app brings nobody. An app team made in the target
  gives its members who hold [every app added later](#every-app-added-later)
  a row too.
- **Invites**: the source's invites naming the team the app leaves are revoked
  once no app of the source is on that team.

The answer is `{ appId, from, org, access, created, added, revokedInvites }`.
A node from before this route answers `404`, which clients take as "not
supported here".

## Capabilities

Beyond their role, a member holds capability bits (`types::caps`, the
`MemberCapabilities` values): organization-wide, with a default the
organization sets, and per direct team row (a new row takes the member's
organization value). Admins and team leads hold them all where they manage.

| Bit | Name | Checked by the node |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `1 << 0` | `CREATE_APP` | Creating an app in the organization (on its team, or org-wide) |
| `1 << 1` | `INVITE_MEMBERS` | Minting an invite (org-wide, or on each team it names, where a team's default capabilities count) |
| `1 << 2` | `JOIN_OPEN_TEAMS` | Inheritance into open teams (granted by default) |
| `1 << 3` | `MANAGE_MEMBERS` | Adding non-lead rows and setting capabilities in a team |
| `1 << 4` | `MANAGE_APPLICATION` | Upgrading the organization's apps (on the app's team, or org-wide), organization-wide upgrade runs, and approving the organization's outbound hosts (org-wide only) |
| `1 << 5` | `CREATE_TEAM` | Creating a top-level team (or moving one to the top) without being an admin |
| `1 << 6` | `DELETE_TEAM` | Deleting a team one does not manage |
| `1 << 7` | `MANAGE_VISIBILITY` | Switching a team between open and restricted |
| `1 << 8` | `MANAGE_METADATA` | Organization and team metadata |
| `1 << 10` | `APPROVE_RELEASES` | Approving (or withdrawing) the mero-tee releases that hold the apps the organization governs: those of the packages it publishes, and its own apps of packages whose key is not bound; and binding a package signing key to the organization (org-wide only: a team grant does not reach it) ([Release approvals](/protocol/governance/#release-approvals)) |
| `1 << 11` | `CREATE_CHILD_TEAM` | Creating a restricted team below a team one writes, without managing it (the creator leads the new team); in an app team's default |

Bit 9 is unused: v1's `MemberCapabilities` spends it on `CAN_AUTHOR_ON_BEHALF`,
so a client that sends v1's `ALL` grants nothing new by accident. A grant is
`POST /orgs/{org}/capabilities { account, capabilities }` by an admin, with
their passkey (as every membership change: roles, removals and leaving, team
rows; `412 CONFIRM_REQUIRED` first); `GET
/orgs/{org}` lists who may approve releases (`releaseApprovers`).

## Invites

`POST /orgs/{org}/invites { role, teams?, ttlSecs?, uses?, allApps? }` returns a token
`inv_…`, after the inviter approves it with a passkey (`412 CONFIRM_REQUIRED`
first; the consent binds `{ inviteKey, role, teams, expiresAt, uses }`). Only
the token's Ed25519 public key is stored (`inviteKey`, the key whose seed is
`SHA-256("calimero.invite.v1\0" ‖ token)`). An invite expires (7 days by default) and
admits `uses` accounts (1 by default). Its role may not exceed the inviter's and
is never `owner`. Only the owner or an admin mints one with `allApps`
([Every app added later](#every-app-added-later)). An inviter below admin needs `INVITE_MEMBERS` on each team the
invite names (organization-wide when it names none), and may name only teams
they see (a member of, or a manager): an organization-wide grant does not open a
restricted team they are not in.
`POST /invites/{token}/redeem`, by a signed-in person (no passkey prompt): the
node signs the redemption with the token's key, which every node checks
where it applies, so a node that saw only the log cannot redeem an invite. It adds them at the invite's
role (or keeps a higher role they already have) and gives them a plain row in
each named team. Only a redemption that does one of those spends a use: an
account already in the organization at the invite's role or above, and in every
team it names (an owner opening their own link, say), gets the organization back
and leaves the use for the person the invite was made for. The last use removes
the invite; redeeming it after that, or after its organization is deleted, is
refused `400 REJECTED` ("this invite was used up, revoked or never existed"), and
past its expiry "this invite has expired". `GET /orgs/{org}/invites` lists the
pending invites the caller may revoke (key, role, teams, uses left, expiry,
creator and creation time; never a token): an admin all, a creator their own,
a team's manager those naming only teams they manage. `DELETE
/orgs/{org}/invites/{inviteKey}` revokes one, with the same rule and a passkey
(`__revoke_invite { inviteKey }`); an invite the caller may not see is `404`.
Invites made before record no creator. A redemption an earlier release logged
replays as that release applied it (it spent a use whatever it changed): the
API marks new ones (`RedeemInvite { free_when_unchanged: true }`). In the same
way, a `CreateInvite`, `RemoveTeamMember` or `DeleteTeam` an earlier release
logged replays without the rules above (the teams the inviter sees, the rows
below, the passkey for apps); the API marks new ones (`teams_seen`, `subtree`,
`apps_need_consent`).

## Recovery

An organization may hold a **recovery key** (`Org::recovery`): an Ed25519 key
whose 32-byte seed its owner keeps offline; the organization stores only the
public half (`publicKey`, base64url). It is set at creation (`POST /orgs
{ name, recoveryKey }`) or by the owner later, with a passkey (`POST
/orgs/{org}/recovery { publicKey }`, consent `org:<org> / __set_recovery_key /
{ publicKey }`; `null` removes it). An organization created before recovery
keys has none until its owner sets one.

Whoever holds the seed hands the organization to the account they are signed
in as: the client signs `"calimero.org-recovery.v1\0" ‖ org ‖ 0 ‖ account ‖ 0 ‖
nonce` with the seed and sends only the signature (`POST
/orgs/{org}/recovery/start { nonce, signature }`). Every node checks it
against the organization's current key where `StartRecovery` applies and
spends it, so a node that saw the log cannot redirect or replay it. The
recovery then waits `RECOVERY_DELAY_SECS` (72 hours, judged at the commands'
own times); members see it as `recovery.pending` on `GET /orgs/{org}`. The
owner may cancel it with a passkey (`__cancel_recovery { account }`), and
setting a new key cancels it too. Once the delay has passed, the account it
names finishes it with its own passkey (`__finish_recovery { org }`): it
becomes the owner, every previous owner leaves the organization and its teams,
and the apps they owned in it become the new owner's. One recovery runs at a
time (`409 RECOVERY_PENDING`). Recovery never changes an account's passkeys:
the previous owner's account and its other organizations are as they were.
