Every app instance is in exactly one **organization**: one its creator is in,
or one made with it, as its package's manifest says ([Where an instance
goes](#where-an-instance-goes)). An organization
holds members with roles, nested **teams** (an **app team** per package it
uses, with restricted child teams below for private channels) and the app
instances, each with a list of grants that names who reaches it.

<OrgStructure />

Access has two layers. The node decides **who reaches an app at all**, from the
app's grants and the system state, on every request. Everything finer —
who may edit which record — is the app's own code, which can read the caller's
standing with `env::org()`, `env::org_role(account)` and `env::in_team(account, team)` ([Who a run acts as](/protocol/authorship/#the-org-the-app-sees)).

Every rule on this page is enforced by the system group's state machine when
the command applies ([The system group](/protocol/system-group/)); the API
checks most of them first so a refused request records nothing.

## App access

An app instance's access is a **list of grants** (`types::Grant`, kept as
`types::Access`), set at creation (`POST /apps { grants }`; absent: `[org]`) and replaced with
`POST /apps/{app}/access { grants }` by its owner, an organization admin, or
whoever manages its teams' apps (`MANAGE_APPLICATION` on each team it admits,
which a team's leads hold, before and after the change); a list with no team
needs `MANAGE_APPLICATION` organization-wide. Each grant is one way in, judged
on its own, so an instance can be "team X plus two partners from outside":

| Grant | Who it admits |
| --- | --- |
| `org` | Every member of the app's organization; a guest of the organization only while it has no app team. Not beside a `team` grant, which it covers |
| `team { team }` | The team's members (direct rows, and inheritance into an open team); at most 16 teams |
| `account { account, scope? }` | One person. A member of the organization is a **named member**; anyone else is a [guest](#guests), admitted only where the instance's release and its organization allow guests. For an instance of a package with an app team in its organization, a named member must also be in that app team. `scope` is the app's own (below); at most 1 000 accounts |
| `anyone` | Any signed-in account, a member of the organization or not. Never a caller without an account (`401`): anonymous visitors come in only through public links |

No grant is the owner alone. The owner reaches their app too, except that an
instance whose only member grants are teams is reached by its owner only while
they are in one of them (or named). A list with a grant twice, an unknown team or
account, `org` beside a team, or a scope over 256 bytes is refused where it
applies. Grants are written from cluster version 1.0.0-rc.33
(`Feature::AccessGrants`; `409 FEATURE_NOT_ACTIVE` before). `GET /apps/{app}`
answers them as `grants`.

<AccessGrants />

Whatever changes an instance's grants keeps the rest: giving an `org` instance
an app team (`ScopeApps`) replaces its `org` grant with the app team's,
deleting a team takes the instances it alone admitted
and the team's grant from the others (or keeps them too, without its grant,
with `keepApps`, below), and moving or handing an instance to
another organization turns its team grant into the target's app team or `org`.

The owner does not always reach their app. An organization's app is
reached only by the organization's current members and its guests: its owner and
the named members stop reaching it, and receiving its events, once they
leave or are removed (the grants keep their names, so being added back to the
organization restores it). An app on a team is reached by its owner only while
they are in that team, as `owner_in_org` asks of the organization: an owner who
leaves the team, is removed from it, or loses it when an open team is closed
without them, stops reaching it (a person removed from Chat's app team no
longer reaches the channels they created), and has it back once they are in
the team again. Likewise an instance only named people reach (a DM, a private
channel) of a package that has an app team in its organization is reached only
by those of its owner and named members who are in that app team (`SystemState::app_team_of`):
someone removed from Chat's team, who leaves it, or who loses it when it
closes, is out of Chat's DMs too, and has them back once in the team again. An
instance of a package with no app team there keeps its names alone. A
caller who does not reach it gets `404`,
so its existence is not disclosed; with no credential, `401`. The check reads
fresh system state, so removing someone from an organization or team holds on
every node before their next request ([Reads of the system
state](/protocol/system-group/#reading-the-system-state)).

An organization's app is **managed** by its owner only while they are a member
of the organization (`member` or above, as owning an app there takes;
`SystemState::owner_acts`). An owner who leaves, is removed or is made a guest
keeps their name on the record but none of its powers: changing its access
(so they cannot grant `anyone` to reach it again), its metadata, transferring
or deleting it, upgrading it or making an upgrade key for it, uploading files
to it or deleting its blobs. The organization's admins (and its owner) still
transfer it to a member or delete it, so an organization cleans up an app whose
owner left; `POST /apps/{app}/transfer` is open to them too, with their own
passkey. Likewise, an app on
a team is managed by its owner only while they are in that team: out of it,
they keep their name on it and none of the powers above, and the
organization's admins (or whoever manages the team's apps) transfer it. The
rules are judged where each command applies; `SetAccess`, `SetAppMetadata`,
`TransferApp`, `DeleteApp`, `UpgradeApp` and `CreateApiKey` entries an earlier
release logged replay as that release judged them (ownership alone, and no
admin transfer, without `owner_in_org`; the owner without the team, without
`owner_in_team`); the API marks new ones.

A member whose **direct row** in the app's team is **read-only** still reaches
the app, but only its views run for them: any other call is refused with `403`
before it runs. A blob upload by them is refused
before it starts.

## Organizations

`POST /orgs { name, capabilities? }` creates an organization with its creator as **owner**.
`capabilities` (bits) sets what members can do unless set for them
(`defaultCapabilities`), in the same `CreateOrg` entry, so an app need not
follow with a second call (`409 FEATURE_NOT_ACTIVE` below cluster version
1.0.0-rc.29); absent, a new organization's default stands.
Its name is one line of 1 to 64 characters with no space at either end
(`400` otherwise). The owner or an admin renames it, with a passkey: `POST
/orgs/{org}/name { name }` (`412 CONFIRM_REQUIRED` first; the consent is
`org:<org> / __rename_org / { name }`, `SystemCmd::RenameOrg`), answered
with `{ orgId, name }`; a member below admin gets `403`, and someone outside
it `404`. Nothing keeps a copy of the name, so `GET /orgs/{org}`, the `orgs`
of `/auth/me` and an invite redeemed after the rename all answer the new
one. A name is checked where the command applies: a `CreateOrg` an earlier
release logged (without `name_fits`) replays taking any name, and an
organization keeps the name it has until renamed.

### Where an instance goes

Every app is in an organization. An organization is an organization: a person's
own is just one with a single member, its owner. From cluster version
1.0.0-rc.32 (`Feature::OrdinaryOrgs`) a sign-up makes none; an account's
organizations are those it creates (`POST /orgs`, or with an instance below) or
is invited to.

A package's developer says in its signed manifest which organization an instance
goes into (`instanceOrg`, kept on the release's record, `PackageVersion::instance_org`;
`meroctl app pack --instance-org` or `instance-org` in
`[package.metadata.calimero]`):

| `instanceOrg` | `POST /apps` naming `org` | naming none | `newOrg: { name }` |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `standalone` | `400 STANDALONE_INSTANCE` | a new organization, made with the instance | a new one, so named |
| `org` | that organization | the creator's only one; `400 ORG_REQUIRED` with several, `409 NO_ORGANIZATION` with none (create one, or accept an invite) | `400 ORG_INSTANCE` |
| `either` (default, and a raw bundle) | that organization | the creator's only one; with none a new one; `400 ORG_REQUIRED` with several | a new one, so named |

The creator's organizations are those they are a `member` or more of. A new
organization is an ordinary one owned by the creator, approving the release of
the node it was made through and counted against `maxOrgsPerAccount`; it is
named `newOrg.name`, else the instance's name made to fit (one line, at most 64
characters), else the package's id. It is written before the instance (two
entries): a failure after it leaves the organization in place, as a unit's
member made before a failure stays. The people of a standalone instance are
invited into its organization. An instance from a deployment follows its
release's mode the same way; a unit's members always join their instance's
organization. A release recorded before the node kept the mode is read from its
`.mpk`; installing it again records it.

#### Personal organizations

Before 1.0.0-rc.32 (below that cluster version) a
sign-up made the account's **personal organization** in the same entry
(`CreateAccount { personalOrg }`, named `Personal`) and an instance naming no
organization went there. Such an organization keeps its `personal: true` flag
(in `GET /orgs/{org}`, the `orgs` of `/auth/me`), and entries logged before the
version replay as they applied. From the version on it is an organization like
any other: no new one is made (`CreateOrg { personal }` is refused, `409
PERSONAL_ORGS_ENDED`, and a sign-up's seed is dropped), it counts against
`maxOrgsPerAccount` (an account above the limit keeps its organizations; only
creating one more is refused), an app there is transferred only to a member
(as everywhere: `400 REJECTED`), a deployment's report names it, and a consent
request with no scope (`"scope": "account"`) stands for the account's only
organization. One thing stays: an instance there still reaches the accounts it
names without making them members, whatever its package's manifest says about
[guests](#guests).

Apps outside any organization were removed in a hard cutover (1.0.0-rc.20):
data holding one is not read.

Members have one role each, ordered `guest < member < admin < owner`:

| Role | May |
| --- | --- |
| `guest` | Once the organization has app teams, reach only the apps of the teams they have a direct row in (no `org`-access app, no open top-level team by inheritance); before that, its `org`-access apps as before. Always see only the people of their teams |
| `member` | Also own apps in the organization and create top-level teams if they hold `CREATE_TEAM` |
| `admin` | Manage members and their roles (below owner), capabilities, teams, organization metadata; delete the organization's apps; approve releases, upgrade apps and approve outbound hosts (they hold every capability) |
| `owner` | Everything, plus ownership and deleting the organization. Release approvals are also open to `APPROVE_RELEASES` holders, outbound approvals to `MANAGE_APPLICATION` holders (see Capabilities); app upgrades are the owner's and admins' alone |

There is exactly one owner. `SetOrgRole { role: owner }` by the owner moves
ownership and makes the previous owner an admin; the owner cannot be removed,
only replaced. Leaving is removing oneself. Removing a member clears their
capabilities, metadata and every team row they had. `DELETE /orgs/{org}`
(owner, with a passkey step-up) deletes the organization with its teams,
invites and apps, and unbinds the package signing keys it published
([Publishers](/protocol/governance/#publishers)).

An organization also keeps metadata for its apps and frontends to read (admins,
or members holding `MANAGE_METADATA`), per-member metadata (the member or an
admin), the mero-tee releases its `APPROVE_RELEASES` holders approved, the
outbound prefixes its `MANAGE_APPLICATION` holders approved, and its recent
upgrade runs. An organization may also be a **publisher**: a package signing key is
bound to it by the key's own signature (`APPROVE_RELEASES` holders send the
binding), and it then approves releases, and (where the package says so) runs
upgrades, for every instance of that key's packages
([Publishers](/protocol/governance/#publishers)). Each metadata value is at most 16 KiB of JSON. Any
member reads, with `GET /orgs/{org}/apps`, the apps they reach or manage
there with the release each runs and its last upgrade (who, of which kind),
the releases that govern each app, and the releases the cluster's nodes run
([Reading the approvals](/protocol/governance/#reading-the-approvals)).

### Private packages

A package an organization installs as private is that organization's alone on
a shared cluster: only its members list it, install it, create instances of it,
upgrade to it or offer it in a deployment. A member who leaves loses it, and an
install for a second organization shares it with that one too. Making an
instance reachable to others (grants, guests, public links, a deployment's
customers) shares that instance, never the release it runs. The full rule is in
[Who sees a release](/protocol/applications/#who-sees-a-release).

### Members' security

The owner and admins read what tells them a member may be locked out, with
`GET /orgs/{org}/members?detail=security` (their own session on the
cluster's origin; never an API key or a package-bound session). For each
member: the self-set `name` (`memberMetadata.name`), `role`, the `teams` they
are in, `passkeys` (`1`, `2` or `"3+"`: whether they have a backup, not how
many devices they own), `recoveryCode` (set or not), `lastActiveDay` (the UTC
day of their latest sign-in or session exchange; `null` when the node knows
neither) and `sessions` (how many live sessions they hold).

| Reader | Sees | Never |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The account holder | Everything about their own account: passkeys with labels and times, sessions, activity (`/auth/me`, `/auth/sessions`, `/auth/activity`) | - |
| The organization's owner and admins, about its members | The fields above | Credential ids, passkey labels (names people choose for their devices), sign counts and times, sessions, origins, activity, a recovery under way |
| A member below admin | Nothing new (`403`) | as above |
| A deployment's managers, about customer organizations | `memberCount` and `activeMembers30d` per organization (`GET /deployments/{id}/orgs`) | Any account, name or per-member fact |
| The operator | Nothing new: the Ops API has no read path into accounts | Anything per account |

A removed member leaves the listing at once, and someone outside the
organization is answered as if it did not exist (`404`).

## Teams

Teams (`types::Team`; `meroctl`'s *groups*) nest (`parent`), move
(`UpdateTeam { parent }`, never under themselves), and are one of two kinds:

- **Open** (`join: everyone`): whoever reaches the parent team — at the top,
  the organization — is a member too, unless they were denied
  `JOIN_OPEN_TEAMS` there. Membership flows down an unbroken chain of open
  teams.
- **Restricted** (`join: invite`): only direct rows. A restricted team is a
  wall: nothing flows through it.

A **direct row** has a role (`lead`, `member` or `read-only`) and capabilities.
A team's creator leads it. Whoever leads a team, or an ancestor team, or is an
organization admin, **manages** it: renames it, adds and removes members,
creates teams under it, moves it within what they manage, and deletes it. A
member who writes a team (a row that is not read-only, or inheritance into an
open team) and holds `CREATE_CHILD_TEAM` there also creates a **restricted**
team below it, and leads that one: this is how any member of an app's team
makes a private channel. An app team holds `CREATE_CHILD_TEAM` in its default
capabilities, so it is on unless an admin turns it off; a `CreateTeam` entry an
earlier release logged replays with only the parent's managers creating below
it (`member_child_teams`).

- Removing someone from a team, or their leaving it, also removes their direct
  rows in every team below it, so a restricted child team (a private channel
  under an app's team) does not keep them in. Closing an open team (`POST
  /orgs/{org}/teams/{team} { join: invite }`) does the same for whoever loses
  it then, being in it by inheritance alone: their rows below it go, and they
  are not barred, so opening it again lets them back into the team (not into
  the channels). An `UpdateTeam` an earlier release logged replays keeping
  those rows (`close_drops_below`).
- A member who leaves a team, or whom a manager removes, does not come back by
  inheritance (`Team::removed`), so
  leaving an open team sticks. They return only when a manager adds them again
  or they redeem an invite that names the team; they cannot rejoin by
  themselves.
- A member may join an open team they already reach as a plain member row, or
  leave any team themselves.
- One account's rows in several teams of the organization are set in one log
  entry with one passkey approval (`POST /orgs/{org}/members/{account}/teams
  { teams: [{ team, role?, capabilities? }] }`, `SystemCmd::SetTeamMembers`,
  consent `__set_team_members { account, teams }`): Calimero Home's "give all
  apps" gives a member every app team at once. They are taken, or left, the
  same way (`DELETE` with `{ teams: [team] }`, `SystemCmd::RemoveTeamMembers`,
  `__remove_team_members { account, teams }`). Each team is judged as its own
  team-member change would be, in order, and the entry applies all or
  nothing: one team the caller may not change refuses the whole batch. A
  batch names 1 to 64 teams (`MAX_TEAM_BATCH`), each once (`400 BAD_TEAMS`).
- Deleting a team deletes the teams below it and the apps deployed to them:
  every instance whose only member grants are those teams' (its team grants all
  go, and no `org` grant), even one that also names guests or other accounts.
  An instance another team or the organization admits only loses the deleted
  teams' grants. When there are apps, it takes the deleter's passkey: `DELETE
  /orgs/{org}/teams/{team}` answers `412 CONFIRM_REQUIRED` with the `apps` and
  `keepApps` in its details, and the consent (`__delete_team { team, apps }`)
  names exactly those, so an app added since is not deleted on an old approval.
- **Keeping the apps** (`DELETE /orgs/{org}/teams/{team} { keepApps: true }`,
  `meroctl team delete --keep-apps`, `DeleteTeam { keep_apps }`,
  `Feature::KeepTeamApps`, cluster version 1.0.0-rc.33, else `409
  FEATURE_NOT_ACTIVE`): those instances stay. Each loses the deleted teams'
  grants and keeps its others (named members, guests, `anyone`, public links);
  one with no grant left has an empty grant list, which only its owner reaches
  (a `team` instance included). The consent states it
  (`__delete_team { team, apps, keepApps: true }`), and the answer lists them in
  `keptApps` (`apps`, the deleted ones, is empty). Without the flag, or absent
  in an entry an earlier release logged, they are deleted as before.
- Reads are filtered: a team someone neither reaches nor manages is left out of
  `GET /orgs/{org}` and answers `404` on its own route.
- A team keeps metadata the app defines (a folder's name and colour), set by
  those holding `MANAGE_METADATA` for it.
- A team may hold **default capabilities** (`Team::default_capabilities`):
  everyone in the team holds them there on top of their organization-wide
  ones, unless their row sets its own. Set with `POST /orgs/{org}/capabilities
  { team, capabilities }` (no `account`) by whoever manages the team or holds
  `MANAGE_MEMBERS` there, with a passkey (`__set_default_capabilities { team,
  capabilities }`). This is how "members may invite others to this app" is
  switched on per app.

An app deployed with `access: team` must be created by someone who writes that
team (a member whose row is not read-only).

## App teams

An **app team** is a top-level team whose metadata marks a package:
`metadata.app = { package, name }` (`Team::app_package`). An organization has
at most one per package: marking a second is refused. Only an owner or admin
makes, changes or drops the mark: installing an app in an organization is
theirs. The app's instances use `access: team` on its app team, or on a
restricted team below it (a private channel). The organization's **mode** is
derived from its app teams, never stored, and answered as `mode` on
`GET /orgs/{org}` and `GET /orgs/{org}/app-teams`:

| Mode | When | Means |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `legacy` | No app team | Apps use `org` access, as before app teams. Behaves as standalone. |
| `standalone` | Every app team open | Everyone in the organization has every app; members may invite to the app (the team's default `INVITE_MEMBERS`) |
| `shared` | Some app team restricted | Each app lists its people; invites name the app team; only leads and admins invite unless an admin turns on the team's default |

`POST /orgs/{org}/app-teams { apps?: [{ package, name?, instances?, join?, keep?, childTeams? }], shared? }`
(an owner or admin, with a passkey: `__scope_apps { apps, shared }`,
`SystemCmd::ScopeApps`) gives apps their own access lists in one log entry.
For each package (absent: every package with an `org`-access instance in the
organization) it finds or makes the app team (its creator leads a new one),
gives a plain row to every member who reaches the instances now, and moves the
instances (absent: every `org`-access instance of the package) to `access:
team` on it. `shared` (absent: whether the organization then holds two app
teams or more) closes every app team, everyone it held kept as a direct row,
and takes `INVITE_MEMBERS` out of its default. Nobody loses an app. A new
app team's default holds `CREATE_CHILD_TEAM` in either mode (`INVITE_MEMBERS`
too while standalone), so its members make private channels below it. App
teams made before keep the default they had (nothing changes on replay or as a
side effect) until an admin turns it on: a package's `childTeams: true` adds
`CREATE_CHILD_TEAM` to its app team's default and to every row there that sets
its own capabilities (a row added one by one keeps what it got then), and
`childTeams: false` takes it out of both. With `instances: []` the step moves
no instance, and naming `childTeams` (as naming `join`) leaves `shared`,
absent, meaning "no other team", so `POST /orgs/{org}/app-teams { apps:
[{ package, instances: [], childTeams: true }, ...] }` turns it on for every
app team named, with one passkey, and changes nothing else.
A package named that has no release the admin may use (a private release of another
organization) answers `400 REJECTED: no such package`, as one the cluster lacks, before
the passkey and again at apply; a new app team, here or by a move, takes the display
name of the newest release its maker may use, else the package id.

To keep one app apart and leave the others as they are, a package names its
own `join` in the same request: `invite` closes that app team alone, in the
same log entry that fills its rows, and `everyone` opens it again (`shared`,
absent, then changes no other team; `shared: true` with an `everyone` is
refused). A close keeps, by default, everyone who reaches the app then, each
as a direct row. With `keep: [account, ...]` (members of the organization
only) the team's leads and those accounts keep it, each with a row, and
everyone else in it, by a row or by inheritance, loses it, together with their
direct rows in the teams below the app team (its private channels), as a
removal from the team takes them; they are not barred, so opening the team
again lets them back in. Each package's answer names its team's `join` after the step and
how many people it `dropped`. A single team also opens or closes with `POST
/orgs/{org}/teams/{team} { join }` (`MANAGE_VISIBILITY`, an admin's anywhere),
which fills no rows: closing that way, only the existing direct rows stay, and
whoever loses the team loses their rows in the teams below it too.
`GET /orgs/{org}/app-teams` lists, to any member, each app team's package, id,
name, `join`, member count, default capabilities and whether the caller is in
it or manages it (a guest: only their own).

### Every app added later

In an organization that keeps its apps apart, a new app's team holds only the
people named when it is made. A member may instead hold a standing grant,
**every app added later** (`Org::all_apps`, `allApps` on `GET /orgs/{org}`):
each app team made from then on gives them a plain row, whoever else it
names, so they reach every new app, open or closed. That is an app team made
by `ScopeApps` (closed with a `keep` list too: they are kept), one a move
makes in the organization ([Moving an app](#moving-an-app)), and a team an
admin marks as an app team (unless they were removed from that team). It
gives nothing in an app team already there: an admin adds them to those as
anyone else.

The grant is the owner's or an admin's to give, with a passkey: on an invite,
`POST /orgs/{org}/invites { ..., allApps: true }` (the consent's arguments
then name `allApps: true`; `403` below admin), which gives it to whoever
redeems it, and later, `POST /orgs/{org}/all-apps { account, allApps }`
(`__set_all_apps { account, allApps }`, `SystemCmd::SetAllApps`; `false`
stops it, the rows already made stay). A member redeeming an `allApps`
invite gets the grant, which spends a use. It ends with their membership:
leaving, removal, or a recovery that removes them. `ScopeApps`, `MoveApp` and
`UpdateTeam` carry `all_apps_rows`, which the API always sets; entries an
earlier release logged replay making no such rows.

While an organization has app teams, creating the first instance of a package
that has none there is an admin's. In a
`shared` organization a member sees, in `GET /orgs/{org}`, only the people of
the teams they are in and its owner and admins; a guest sees only their teams'
people in every mode; owners and admins see everyone. Calimero Home, the
portal that manages people, invites and apps across an organization, is one
first-party package every cluster installs; it uses these routes and needs
nothing else from the node.

## Moving an app

`POST /apps/{app}/move { toOrg, bringMembers? }` (`SystemCmd::MoveApp`, with a
passkey: `<app> / __move { toOrg, bringMembers }`) moves an app instance to
another organization in one log entry. Its data stays where it is: the same
group, replicas and state. Calimero Home's "Merge organizations" moves every
app of one organization into another this way.

- **Who**: whoever manages the app where it is now (its owner while a member,
  or an admin there), as an admin or the owner of the
  target. Judged on the state of the moment, so a member removed from the
  source, or an admin of the target demoted, is refused.
- **Payer**: its file bytes, compute runs and quotas go to the target. A move
  past the target's file or compute quota is refused (`429 QUOTA_EXCEEDED`),
  as is one to a target that approves none of the releases holding the app now.
- **Grants**: an `org` grant, or a `team` grant on its package's app team,
  becomes a `team` grant on the target's app team of the package. The target's
  is used, or made when missing: open, unless every app team of the target
  is restricted (`shared: true`), where it is restricted like its others; an
  app team an admin closed alone leaves it open. The mover leads a new one,
  whose default capabilities are any new app team's (`CREATE_CHILD_TEAM`, and
  `INVITE_MEMBERS` while open). A `legacy` target, and a raw upload (no
  package), get an `org` grant instead, so moving an app in never changes the
  target's mode. Named people and `anyone` stay as they are, though only the
  target's members (and its guests) reach it from then on; an instance only
  named people reach whose package has a closed app team in the target is
  reached there only through that team (see App access), so its owner and the
  people it brings get a row on it (below).
  An app on any other team (a restricted team
  below the app team, a plain team) is refused: that team's leads, read-only
  rows and capabilities are grants of the source, which the target's admins
  never saw. Give it an `org` or app-team grant first.
- **Owner**: stays, and must be a member of the target once the move is done,
  or the move is refused: an organization's app is owned by one of its members,
  and only a transfer the owner or an admin consents to changes who that is.
  An owner who reaches the app now gets a row on the target's app team, as an
  owner reaches and manages an app on a team only while in it.
- **`bringMembers`**: every member of the source who reaches the app joins the
  target at their role there, capped at `member` (a guest stays a guest; an
  admin comes in as a member, and the target's admins grant more themselves),
  and gets a row on the target's app team: the one an `org` or `team`
  instance moves to, or, for an instance only named people reach (a DM, a
  private channel), its package's app team there when that team is closed, so they
  keep reaching it (an open one needs no row). Someone already in the target
  keeps their role. An app team made in the
  target gives its members who hold [every app added later](#every-app-added-later)
  a row too. A `MoveApp` an earlier release logged (without
  `accounts_in_app_team`) replays giving its named people no such row.
- **Invites**: the source's invites naming the team the app leaves are revoked
  once no app of the source is on that team.

The answer is `{ appId, from, org, grants, created, added, revokedInvites }`.
A node from before this route answers `404`, which clients take as "not
supported here".

## Capabilities

Beyond their role, a member holds capability bits (`types::caps`, the
`MemberCapabilities` values): organization-wide, with a default the
organization sets, and per direct team row (a new row takes the member's
organization value). Admins and team leads hold them all where they manage.

| Bit | Name | Checked by the node |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `1 << 0` | `CREATE_APP` | Creating an app in the organization (on its team, or org-wide) |
| `1 << 1` | `INVITE_MEMBERS` | Minting an invite (org-wide, or on each team it names, where a team's default capabilities count) |
| `1 << 2` | `JOIN_OPEN_TEAMS` | Inheritance into open teams (granted by default) |
| `1 << 3` | `MANAGE_MEMBERS` | Adding non-lead rows and setting capabilities in a team: below admin, only a team the holder sees, and only capabilities the holder has organization-wide (from cluster version 1.0.0-rc.38, `heldTeamGrants`) |
| `1 << 4` | `MANAGE_APPLICATION` | Approving the organization's outbound hosts (org-wide only). Upgrading its apps and its upgrade runs are its owner's and admins' alone from 1.0.0-rc.33 ([App upgrades](/protocol/governance/#app-upgrades)); before, this capability gave them too (on the app's team, or org-wide) |
| `1 << 5` | `CREATE_TEAM` | Creating a top-level team (or moving one to the top) without being an admin |
| `1 << 6` | `DELETE_TEAM` | Deleting a team one does not manage |
| `1 << 7` | `MANAGE_VISIBILITY` | Switching a team between open and restricted |
| `1 << 8` | `MANAGE_METADATA` | Organization and team metadata |
| `1 << 10` | `APPROVE_RELEASES` | Approving (or withdrawing) the mero-tee releases that hold the apps the organization governs: those of the packages it publishes, and its own apps of packages whose key is not bound; and binding a package signing key to the organization (org-wide only: a team grant does not reach it) ([Release approvals](/protocol/governance/#release-approvals)) |
| `1 << 11` | `CREATE_CHILD_TEAM` | Creating a restricted team below a team one writes, without managing it (the creator leads the new team); in an app team's default |

Bit 9 is unused: v1's `MemberCapabilities` spends it on `CAN_AUTHOR_ON_BEHALF`,
so a client that sends v1's `ALL` grants nothing new by accident. A grant is
`POST /orgs/{org}/capabilities { account, capabilities }` by an admin, with
their passkey (as every membership change: roles, removals and leaving, team
rows; `412 CONFIRM_REQUIRED` first); `GET
/orgs/{org}` lists who may approve releases (`releaseApprovers`).

An app's own frontend cannot call these routes; it asks the node's consent page
to mint (`createInvite`) or accept (`acceptInvite`) an invite with the person's
passkey ([The consent page](/protocol/consent-page/#organizations-and-invites)).

`POST /orgs/{org}/invites { role, teams?, ttlSecs?, uses?, allApps?, capabilities? }` returns a token
`inv_…`, after the inviter approves it with a passkey (`412 CONFIRM_REQUIRED`
first; the consent binds `{ inviteKey, role, teams, expiresAt, uses }`). Only
the token's Ed25519 public key is stored (`inviteKey`, the key whose seed is
`SHA-256("calimero.invite.v1\0" ‖ token)`). An invite expires (7 days by default) and
admits `uses` accounts (1 by default). Its role may not exceed the inviter's and
is never `owner`. Only the owner or an admin mints one with `allApps`
([Every app added later](#every-app-added-later)). An inviter below admin needs `INVITE_MEMBERS` on each team the
invite names (organization-wide when it names none), and may name only teams
they see (a member of, or a manager): an organization-wide grant does not open a
restricted team they are not in.
`capabilities` (bits) is what a new member holds as their own setting
(`Org::capabilities`) once they redeem it, in place of the organization's
default; a member already in keeps theirs. An inviter below admin may give
only bits they hold organization-wide (`403 CANNOT_GRANT_UNHELD`, judged by the
log too, so every node agrees; bits no capability has are `400 UNKNOWN_CAPABILITIES`); the owner and admins hold every bit. The consent's arguments
then name `capabilities`, and the invite's earlier shape (none) is unchanged
(`409 FEATURE_NOT_ACTIVE` below cluster version 1.0.0-rc.29).
`POST /invites/{token}/redeem`, by a signed-in person (no passkey prompt): the
node signs the redemption with the token's key, which every node checks
where it applies, so a node that saw only the log cannot redeem an invite. It adds them at the invite's
role (or keeps a higher role they already have) and gives them a plain row in
each named team. Only a redemption that does one of those spends a use: an
account already in the organization at the invite's role or above, and in every
team it names (an owner opening their own link, say), gets the organization back
and leaves the use for the person the invite was made for. The last use removes
the invite; redeeming it after that, or after its organization is deleted, is
refused `400 REJECTED` ("this invite was used up, revoked or never existed"), and
past its expiry "this invite has expired". `GET /orgs/{org}/invites` lists the
pending invites the caller may revoke (key, role, teams, uses left, expiry,
creator and creation time; never a token): an admin all, a creator their own,
a team's manager those naming only teams they manage. `DELETE
/orgs/{org}/invites/{inviteKey}` revokes one, with the same rule and a passkey
(`__revoke_invite { inviteKey }`); an invite the caller may not see is `404`.
Invites made before record no creator. A redemption an earlier release logged
replays as that release applied it (it spent a use whatever it changed): the
API marks new ones (`RedeemInvite { free_when_unchanged: true }`). In the same
way, a `CreateInvite`, `RemoveTeamMember` or `DeleteTeam` an earlier release
logged replays without the rules above (the teams the inviter sees, the rows
below, the passkey for apps); the API marks new ones (`teams_seen`, `subtree`,
`apps_need_consent`).

## Guests

A **guest** of an instance is an account invited to that one instance (and the
apps its unit created for it) without joining its organization: an `account`
grant naming someone who is not a member. From cluster version 1.0.0-rc.33 (`Feature::Guests`) a
guest reaches the instance when both switches allow guests, the stricter
winning:

| Switch | Where | Default |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The developer's | the release's signed manifest, `guests: "allowed" \| "forbidden"` (`meroctl app pack --guests`; kept on `PackageVersion::guests`: a release recorded before the node kept it, a raw bundle, takes none until the same `.mpk` is installed again) | `forbidden` |
| The organization's | `allowGuests`, an admin policy set by its owner or an admin with a passkey: `POST /orgs/{org}/settings { allowGuests }` (`org:<org> / __set_org_settings / settings`, `SystemCmd::SetOrgSettings`); `GET /orgs/{org}` shows `allowGuests: false` when off | allowed |

A named account that is a member is a named member, with the member semantics
above (its app team included). Access is judged fresh on every request, so
taking a guest's grant away (`POST /apps/{app}/access`, by whoever may change
the instance's access), turning the organization's switch off, or an upgrade to
a release that forbids guests holds from the guest's next request. An instance
in an organization made as a personal one before 1.0.0-rc.32 keeps its named
accounts' reach whatever the switches say ([Personal
organizations](#personal-organizations)).

What a guest may do: what any account that reaches the instance may (call its
methods and views, receive its events, read and upload its blobs, as a named
member could), nothing more. They are not a member: they see none of the
organization's members, teams, other apps, invites, settings, usage or reports
(`404` as to a stranger), the app's code sees no role for them
(`env::org_role` is `None`), and they never manage the instance, approve its
upgrades, set its secrets, list or export its sealed files (`/files` is the
organization's members' and its owner's), or delegate, link or move it.
`GET /apps/{app}` shows a guest only their own grant.
Unit members: a guest of an instance reaches the apps its unit created for it
(`AppRecord::links`, transitively), judged by the instance's grants and switches;
an attached app is not part of it.

### Instance invites

An instance's managers (whoever may change its access) invite people with a
link, like an organization invite but for one instance, on any instance: `POST
/apps/{app}/invites { ttlSecs?, uses?, scope? }` returns a token `inv_…` once
(`412 CONFIRM_REQUIRED` first; the consent is `<app> / __create_app_invite /
{ inviteKey, expiresAt, uses, scope? }`; only the token's Ed25519 public key is
logged, `SystemCmd::CreateAppInvite`, which needs `Feature::AccessGrants` too).
It is made whatever the guest switches say now: whether a guest may come in is
judged when someone redeems it. `POST
/invites/{token}/redeem` (the same route as an organization invite; the node
tells them apart) adds an `account` grant for the redeemer, with the invite's
`scope` (`RedeemAppInvite`, signed with the token's key): a guest, or a named
member when they are a member of the organization, never a new member. It answers `{ app, org, member }`; one already named spends no use. A
member of the organization (`member` or above) is always named; anyone else is
admitted as a guest only while the instance's release says `guests: allowed`
and the organization's `allowGuests` is on, judged on the state the redemption
applies to, so a switch turned off after the invite was made holds (`409
GUESTS_NOT_ALLOWED`; the invite and its uses stay). `GET
/apps/{app}/invites` lists the pending ones (key, uses left, expiry, creator,
scope; never a token) and `DELETE /apps/{app}/invites/{inviteKey}` revokes one
with a passkey (`__revoke_app_invite { inviteKey }`). Deleting the instance
drops its invites. Bounds: `MAX_APP_INVITES_PER_APP` (64) pending per instance,
`MAX_APP_INVITES` (10 000) in the cluster, `MAX_APP_INVITE_USES` (1 000) uses
and `MAX_APP_INVITE_TTL_SECS` (90 days) each, `MAX_ACCOUNT_GRANTS` (1 000) names
per instance and `MAX_GRANT_SCOPE_BYTES` (256) per scope. The consent page's
`acceptInvite` shows an instance invite as joining the app of a package in an
organization, as a guest or, to a member, as the member they are.

### The platform admits, the app scopes

The node decides only who reaches an instance. Anything finer, such as which
channel of a chat a partner may read, is the app's own rule, but the platform
carries it for the app: an invite, and the `account` grant it makes, may carry a
small opaque JSON `scope` the app defines (`{"channel":"partners"}`), which the
node stores, shows to the instance's managers and never reads. The app reads the
caller's scope with `env::grant_scope()` (WIT `context.grant-scope`, 0.5.6: the
grant on the instance, or on the instance whose unit made it; none for anyone
not named, and in a compute run). To let a partner into one channel of a chat,
its managers invite them with that scope; the chat reads it and keeps them to
that channel. A grant without a scope is the app's whole. Changing the scope is
changing the grant (`POST /apps/{app}/access`). A component built before 0.5.6
cannot read a scope, so it admits the person to all of it.

## Public links

A **public link** lets anyone holding its token call one instance's
`#[app::public]` methods as an anonymous **visitor**, without an account and
whatever the instance's other grants (from cluster version 1.0.0-rc.32,
`Feature::PublicLinks`). It is the third kind of grant beside member and named
grants: a `link` grant in the instance's list (`Grant::Link`: its key, label,
expiry, rate, creator), which admits no account, only its visitors. It is made
and revoked only on its own routes, each with a passkey; `POST
/apps/{app}/access` keeps an instance's links as they are, and refuses one it does not already hold. `GET /apps/{app}` shows
link grants only to whoever may change the instance's access. Whoever may change
the instance's access makes one with a passkey: `POST /apps/{app}/public-links { label?, expiresAt?, ratePerMinute? }`
(`412 CONFIRM_REQUIRED` first; the consent is `<app> / __create_public_link /
{ linkKey, label, expiresAt, ratePerMinute }`) answers `{ link, token }` once;
only the token's Ed25519 public key (`link`) is logged, so a node that saw the
log cannot let anyone in. `GET /apps/{app}/public-links` lists them (never a
token) and `DELETE /apps/{app}/public-links/{link}` revokes one with a passkey.
A link lasts until revoked unless it names `expiresAt`; an instance holds at
most 16 (`MAX_PUBLIC_LINKS_PER_APP`); deleting the instance drops them. The
organization's owner or an admin turns links off with `POST /orgs/{org}/settings
{ allowPublicLinks: false }`: no new link, and existing ones admit nobody at the
visitor's next call (`allowPublicLinks` in `GET /orgs/{org}`).

A visitor holds its own Ed25519 key (a browser keeps its seed in local
storage) and sends, on every request, `Authorization: Bearer
vis1.<token>.<key>.<at>.<signature>`: its public key (64 hex) and its signature
over `"calimero.visitor.v1\0" ‖ link ‖ 0 ‖ key ‖ 0 ‖ at`, `at` within 5 minutes
of the node's clock (mero-js `client.useVisitor({ link, seed })`, the Rust
client's `use_visitor`, mero-py's `client.use_visitor(link=..., seed=...)`,
MeroKit's and mero-kotlin's `useVisitor`). Nothing about a visitor is logged. The node admits it
only to that instance's calls and views of `#[app::public]` methods
(`403` for any other method, `403 VISITOR_ONLY` on every other route, `404` for
another app, `401` once the link is revoked, expired or turned off), at most
`ratePerMinute` calls a minute per visitor on each node (`429 TOO_MANY_VISITS`;
default 60, at most 600), besides a method's own `rate`. The app sees
`Caller::Visitor { link, key }` (WIT `context.visitor`, 0.5.6; a component
built before sees `Caller::Public`), so it can tell visitors apart and count
one answer per visitor; it has no org role, and the SDK's guards refuse it.

## Recovery

An organization may hold a **recovery key** (`Org::recovery`): an Ed25519 key
whose 32-byte seed its owner keeps offline; the organization stores only the
public half (`publicKey`, base64url). It is set at creation (`POST /orgs
{ name, recoveryKey }`) or by the owner later, with a passkey (`POST
/orgs/{org}/recovery { publicKey }`, consent `org:<org> / __set_recovery_key /
{ publicKey }`; `null` removes it). An organization created before recovery
keys has none until its owner sets one.

An organization's owner is an account, and an account acts only with its own
passkeys. An owner who loses every passkey can no longer act for the
organization, and nobody can act for them: an admin cannot change ownership,
no Ops route touches an organization, and every decision carries its maker's
proof. Recovery moves the organization's **ownership** to another account, the
one the person signs up with a new passkey, after a delay during which the
current owner can cancel. It acts on the organization only: one
organization's seed reaches nothing outside it (not the owner's other
organizations), and an account that owns several sets
a key on each. To get the account itself back, see
[Account recovery](/protocol/identities/#account-recovery).

<OrgRecoveryTimeline />

| Step | Route | Who | Proof logged | Effect where it applies |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Set or rotate | `POST /orgs/{org}/recovery { publicKey }` (`null` removes; `412` first) | The owner | Passkey consent over `org:<org> / __set_recovery_key / { publicKey }` | `SetRecoveryKey`: replaces the key and cancels a pending recovery |
| Set at creation | `POST /orgs { name, recoveryKey }` | The creator | The session, as for any new organization | The new organization holds the key |
| Start | `POST /orgs/{org}/recovery/start { nonce, signature }` | Any signed-in account (the new one) | The seed's signature over `recovery_message(org, account, nonce)` | `StartRecovery`: `pending = { account, startedAt, readyAt = startedAt + 72 h }` |
| Cancel | `POST /orgs/{org}/recovery/cancel` (`412` first) | The owner | Passkey consent over `__cancel_recovery / { account }` | `CancelRecovery`: nothing pending |
| Finish | `POST /orgs/{org}/recovery/finish` (`412` first) | The pending account, at or after `readyAt` | Passkey consent over `__finish_recovery / { org }` | `FinishRecovery`: the account becomes the owner; every previous owner leaves the organization and its teams, and the apps they owned in it become the new owner's |

`recovery_message` is `"calimero.org-recovery.v1\0" ‖ org ‖ 0 ‖ account ‖ 0 ‖
nonce` (`calimero_identity::recovery_message`). The client derives the public
key and signs on the person's machine, and only the signature travels. It binds
the account the organization goes to, so a node that saw the log cannot
redirect it, and it is spent once (`used_proofs`). It is checked against the
organization's **current** key, at the API and again where the command
applies, on every node, so rotating the key makes every signature under the old
one fail.

**Limits.** `RECOVERY_DELAY_SECS` (72 hours) is judged at the commands' own
times (stamped by the node that takes the request, replicated with it), never
at apply. One pending recovery per organization: a second start is `409
RECOVERY_PENDING` until the owner cancels or the pending one finishes, so a
seed holder cannot replace a pending recovery with their own. The nonce is at
most `MAX_RECOVERY_NONCE_BYTES` (64); the key and signature text are bounded
before decoding (`MAX_RECOVERY_KEY_CHARS`, 44; `MAX_RECOVERY_SIGNATURE_CHARS`,
88). A wrong seed is refused at the API before anything is logged, and no
counter limits attempts: guessing a 256-bit seed is not a threat a counter
changes. An unknown organization, one with no key and a wrong signature all
answer `403 WRONG_RECOVERY_KEY`, so an outsider learns nothing about which
organizations exist or hold a key.

**What members see.** `GET /orgs/{org}` shows members `recovery: { publicKey,
setAt, pending }` (absent when no key is set), so a client can warn every
member, the owner first, while a recovery is pending. The recovering account is
not a member yet; it reads the pending recovery's times in the answers of
`start` and `finish`. Nothing about recovery is in the Ops API or
`/ops/metrics`.

| Who | Can they recover the organization? | What limits it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The operator | No | The seed never reaches a node; no Ops route starts, cancels or finishes a recovery, and the operator's signature is not a recovery proof |
| A node, or anyone reading the log | No | The log holds the public key and spent signatures, each bound to one account |
| An admin or member | No | Setting, rotating and cancelling need the owner's passkey; starting needs the seed |
| An account outside the organization | Only with the seed | The seed is the credential |
| Someone who stole the seed | Yes, unless the owner acts within 72 hours | Every member sees the pending recovery; the owner cancels with a passkey and rotates the key |
| The owner, every passkey lost | Yes | Sign up with a new passkey, start with the seed, wait 72 hours, finish |
| The owner, passkeys and seed lost | No | Nobody can |

A thief who already finished a recovery owns the organization; the delay and
the warning to members are the defence. Recovery never changes an account's
passkeys: the previous owner's account and its other organizations are as they
were. Organizations written before recovery keys hold none
(`Org::recovery` is left out when unset) until their owner sets one.

Recovering the **data** after every mero-kms replica is lost is a different
mechanism: the recovery disk and its backup keys, below.

## Backup key

The recovery disk (`merod --recovery-dir`) writes, every 5 minutes, on every
node that holds an app, the app's state, the code it runs and its holder's
slice of the system state, each sealed to the holder's X25519 public key (HPKE)
and signed by the node's attested receipt key
([Recovery files](/protocol/storage/#recovery-files)). The **backup holder**
is whoever upgrades the app (`SystemState::backup_holder`): the operator where
it holds app upgrades, else the app's organization, also for a package that
declares `upgrades: publisher`. An account's own apps are in an organization
like any other app. No node ever holds a private half.

| Holder | Read | Set |
| --- | --- | --- |
| An organization | `GET /orgs/{org}/backup-key` (any member) | `POST /orgs/{org}/backup-key { publicKey }` by its owner or an admin (an org-wide `MANAGE_APPLICATION` holder below cluster version 1.0.0-rc.38, `backupKeyApprovers`), with a passkey (`org:<org> / __set_backup_key / { publicKey }`, `412` first) |
| The operator | `GET /ops/backup-key` | `POST /ops/backup-key { publicKey }`, signed with the operator key (`operator / __set_backup_key / { publicKey }`; [The operator](/protocol/operator/#the-recovery-disk)) |

`publicKey` is 64 lowercase hex characters (`400 BAD_BACKUP_KEY` otherwise).
The answer is `{ holder, key: { publicKey, setAt, setBy } | null, apps }`,
`apps` being how many apps' backups open with it. Setting a key is the
`SetBackupKey` command, gated by the `backupKeys` feature (1.0.0-rc.20,
[The cluster version](/protocol/upgrades/#the-cluster-version)); the owner-side
routes answer `403` where the operator holds app upgrades. Until a key is set,
nothing is written for that holder's apps.

An organization's apps come back on a cluster that does not hold them with
`POST /recover/holder`: the holder's own device opens the data keys of its
files with its private key and sends them over the sealed channel, and the
operator admits the import with its signature (`SystemCmd::ImportHolder`). The
organization, its people (who sign in with the passkeys they had), its apps and
its backup key come back; it is refused where any of them is on the cluster
already ([Recover after every key service is gone](/operate/runbooks/#recover-after-every-key-service-is-gone)).

## In `calimero-node`

Where the node implements this chapter, by the names in its code: paths are under `crates/node/src`
unless another crate is named. The design and its reasons are above; a module's own detail is in its
`//!` doc, and the node's mental model is [`crates/node/AGENTS.md`](https://github.com/calimero-network/core/blob/master/crates/node/AGENTS.md).

### Every app is in an organization

Code: `AppRecord::org`, required; hard cutover in 1.0.0-rc.20: a record or entry without one is not
read, so clusters are recreated.

**Ordinary organizations** (`Feature::OrdinaryOrgs`, rc.32; `SystemState::ordinary_orgs`, over
`cluster_brings`; docs: protocol organizations "Where an instance goes"): from that logged version a
sign-up makes no organization (`register/finish` sends no `CreateAccount { personal_org }` and apply
drops one an entry carries), and `POST /apps` places an instance by its release's signed
`instanceOrg` (`calimero_bundle::InstanceOrg`: `standalone` | `org` | `either`, absent = `either`;
kept on `PackageVersion::instance_org`, recorded by `InstallPackage` only once the feature is active
and filled in by a reinstall, else read from the `.mpk`, `api::apps::instance_org_of`):
`api::apps::instance_org` answers the org named, the creator's only one (`SystemState::orgs_of`,
`member` or above), or a new ordinary one it writes first (`CreateOrg`, named `newOrg.name`, else
the instance's name made to fit, `standalone_org_name`); refusals `400 ORG_REQUIRED`, `409
NO_ORGANIZATION`, `400 STANDALONE_INSTANCE`, `400 ORG_INSTANCE`.
A unit's members join their instance's org; a deployment's instance follows its release's mode.
The mode is judged by the API only (apply sees an ordinary `CreateOrg` and `CreateApp`).

**Personal organizations** (`Org::personal`, made below that version): a sign-up made the account's
in the same entry (`CreateAccount { personal_org: PersonalOrgSeed }`, `orgs::new_org`, named
`PERSONAL_ORG_NAME`), and `POST /apps` with no `org` created the instance there
(`api::apps::personal_org`).
Below the version every rule applies as logged; from it, `SystemState::acts_personal` is false for
all of them: `CreateOrg { personal }` is refused (`NO_PERSONAL_ORGS`, `409 PERSONAL_ORGS_ENDED`),
every owned org counts against `maxOrgsPerAccount`, `TransferApp` hands an app only to a member
(`NEW_OWNER_NOT_A_MEMBER`, `400 REJECTED`, checked by `app_transfer` before the passkey),
`personal_org_of` answers none, `deployment_orgs` names it, the consent page's `scope: account` is
the account's only org, and `/auth/apps` reads that org's approvals.
`may_reach` still lets an instance in an org flagged `personal` reach the accounts it names without
membership.
There are no account approvals (`ReleaseScope` is `Org` or `Cluster`); `release_may_serve_account`
reads the account's organizations.

### Access grants

Code: `Feature::AccessGrants`, rc.33; `types::Access`, a list of `types::Grant`, serialized as the
record's `grants`; `SystemState::grants_reach`, `check_grants`, `manages_access`, `manages_grants`;
docs: protocol organizations "App access".

An instance's access is a list of grants and nothing else, `org`, `team { team }` (at most
`MAX_TEAM_GRANTS`), `account { account, scope? }` (a named member or a guest, at most
`MAX_ACCOUNT_GRANTS`; `scope` opaque JSON of at most `MAX_GRANT_SCOPE_BYTES` the app defines) and
`anyone` (any signed-in account; no grant admits a caller without one), each judged on its own in
`may_reach` (`grants_reach`); no grant is the owner alone, and an owner of an instance only teams
admit reaches it while in one of them.
Every `SetAccess` and a `CreateApp` with any grant need the feature (`SystemCmd::features`) and
apply only a list `check_grants` accepts (no grant twice, `org` not beside a team, known teams and
accounts, scopes within the limit).
The sites that read access go through `Access` helpers: `teams`/`the_team` (team-scoped management,
`may_set_access` and `may_upgrade` through `manages_access`, `owner_in_team`, read-only rows, team
runs, the report's `team`), `org_wide` (`ScopeApps`, which swaps the `org` grant for the app team's,
`onto_team`), `named_only` (`MoveApp`'s `accounts_in_app_team`), `teams_to_org` (`MoveApp`,
`TransferApp`), `anyone` (listing, `member_apps`).
`DeleteTeam` takes the instances whose every team grant goes (`team_apps`) and strips the team's
grants from the rest (`without_teams`); with `keep_apps` (`Feature::KeepTeamApps`, rc.33; API
`keepApps`, stated in the consent, `delete_team_args`) it keeps them too, without the team's grants
(a list left empty: the owner alone), answered as `keptApps`; absent, they are deleted.
The API takes `grants` on `POST /apps` (absent: `[org]`; a request still naming the old `access` is
refused `400 ACCESS_REMOVED`, never read as no grants) and `POST /apps/{app}/access`, and answers
them on `GET /apps/{app}` and a move.
A hard cutover (#5194, rc.33, `hard-cutover:` in `.github/upgrade-from`): no earlier access shape is
read, so clusters are recreated.

### Guests

Code: `Feature::Guests`, rc.33; `SystemState::guest_reaches`, `guests_on`, `guests_allowed`,
`guest_holder`, `is_guest`, `manages_grants`; docs: protocol organizations "Guests".

An account an instance names (an `account` grant) that is not a member of its organization reaches
it (and its unit's members: `guest_holder` walks `unit_parent` up at most `MAX_UNIT_LINKS_UP`) from
the logged version on, while the release it runs says `guests: allowed`
(`calimero_bundle::GuestPolicy`, absent = forbidden; kept on `PackageVersion::guests`, recorded only
under the feature and filled in by a reinstall; a release recorded without it, or a raw bundle,
takes none) and its organization's `Org::allow_guests` is on (default; `SetOrgSettings { settings:
OrgSettings }`, owner or admin, consent `org:<org> / __set_org_settings / settings`, `POST
/orgs/{org}/settings`).
Judged in `may_reach` before the membership check, so every gated route and `receives` follow it; a
guest is no member, so org routes, `/files`, management and upgrades refuse them; `GET /apps/{app}`
shows a guest only their own grant (`Access::only`).
Grants change with `SetAccess` by whoever may change the access (`manages_grants`), or by an
instance invite, on any instance (`SystemState::app_invites`, `types::AppInvite` under the token's
Ed25519 key, with an optional `scope`): `CreateAppInvite` (consent `<app> / __create_app_invite /
app_invite_args`, `scope` stated only when set; made whatever the guest switches say, which are
judged at redemption; bounds `MAX_APP_INVITES_PER_APP`, `MAX_APP_INVITES`, `MAX_APP_INVITE_USES`,
`MAX_APP_INVITE_TTL_SECS`), `RevokeAppInvite` (`__revoke_app_invite`), `RedeemAppInvite` (the token
key's `sign_invite_redeem`; adds an `account` grant with the invite's scope, `Access::naming`; a
guest only while guests are allowed, a member always, answered `member`; free when named already;
both invite commands need `Feature::AccessGrants` too); `remove_app` drops them.
Routes `POST|GET /apps/{app}/invites`, `DELETE /apps/{app}/invites/{key}`; the redemption is `POST
/invites/{token}/redeem` (`api::orgs::redeem_invite` tells the kinds apart), and the consent page's
`acceptInvite` shows an instance invite as joining that app as a guest, or as the member they are
(`consent_page::app_invite_model`).
An organization flagged `personal` keeps its named accounts' reach whatever the switches say.

### Public links and visitors

Code: `Feature::PublicLinks`, rc.33; `api/public_links.rs`, `types::Grant::Link`,
`types::LinkGrant`, `SystemState::public_link`, `public_link_admits`, `public_link_count`; docs:
protocol organizations "Public links".

A link is a grant of the instance (`Grant::Link`, in its `Access`, `Access::with_link`), which
admits no account in `may_reach`, only its visitors; `public_link` finds it by key among the
instances (a visitor's request and making one are the only callers).
`SetAccess` keeps an instance's links as they are (`Access::keeping_links`) and refuses one it does
not hold, and `check_grants` refuses any in a list, so a link is made only with its consent; `GET
/apps/{app}` shows link grants only to whoever may change the access (`Access::without_links`).
Whoever may change an instance's access makes a link with a passkey (`CreatePublicLink`, consent
`<app> / __create_public_link / public_link_args`, `POST /apps/{app}/public-links { label?,
expiresAt?, ratePerMinute? }` → `{ link, token }` once; the log keeps
`proof::public_link_key(token)`), lists them (`GET`, no token) and revokes them (`RevokePublicLink`,
`__revoke_public_link`); bounds `MAX_PUBLIC_LINKS_PER_APP`, `MAX_PUBLIC_LINKS`,
`MAX_PUBLIC_LINK_LABEL_CHARS`, `MAX_PUBLIC_LINK_RATE`; no forced expiry; they go with their
instance; the organization's `allowPublicLinks` (`SetOrgSettings`) off refuses new links and admits
nobody.
A visitor sends `Bearer vis1.<token>.<key>.<at>.<sig>`, its own Ed25519 key over
`proof::visitor_message` within `VISITOR_PROOF_WINDOW_SECS` (`proof::verify_visitor`); nothing about
it is logged.
`api::credential` makes it `Caller::Visitor { link, key }`, `principal` refuses it on every route
(`403 VISITOR_ONLY`), and only `gate` admits it (`public_links::visitor_gate`): a method (never `*`
or a reserved one) of its link's instance, the link admitted on fresh state, the cluster version
bringing the feature (the caller is in the app's log entry), and `VisitorLimits` (a token bucket per
link and visitor key, the link's `ratePerMinute`, `MAX_TRACKED_VISITORS`, `429 TOO_MANY_VISITS`).
`abi_check` refuses a method that is not `public` (`403`), and the app's leader judges the link
again before the call (`NOT_REACHED`).
The component sees `public` through the WIT `caller` and the visitor through `context.visitor`
(0.5.6).
The caller's grant scope reaches the component through `context.grant-scope` (0.5.6,
`Directory::grant_scope`, `SystemState::grant_scope`: the person's `account` grant on the instance
or on the instance whose unit made it).

### An org app's owner, while a member

Code: `SystemState::owner_acts`.

An organization's app gives its owner the owner's powers (access, metadata, transfer, delete,
upgrade, upgrade keys, files, blob deletes, usage) only while they are a member (`member` or above)
of its organization; its admins keep transfer and delete (`manages_app`).
Judged at apply; `SetAccess`, `SetAppMetadata`, `TransferApp`, `DeleteApp`, `UpgradeApp` and
`CreateApiKey` carry `owner_in_org`, which the API always sets; absent in an entry an earlier
release logged, which replays judging ownership alone.

### An app on a team, its owner while in the team

Code: `SystemState::owner_acts`, `may_reach`.

An app only teams admit is reached and managed by its owner only while they are in one of them
(`Org::in_team`), so an owner kicked from, leaving, or closed out of an app's team (or a private
channel) loses the channels they created there; admins transfer them.
The six owner commands above carry `owner_in_team`, which the API always sets; absent, an entry
replays judging the owner without the team (`OwnerRule`).

### Teams

Code: `types::Org`.

Nested (`parent`, moved with `UpdateTeam`), open (`TeamJoin::Everyone`: whoever reaches the parent,
the organization at the top, is a member, unless denied `caps::JOIN_OPEN_TEAMS`) or restricted
(direct rows only).
A direct row has a role (`TeamRole`: lead, member, read-only) and capabilities (`caps`,
`MemberCapabilities` bits; the organization keeps a default).
An app deployed with `access: team` is reached by the team's members; a member whose direct row
there is read-only runs only its views, any other call refused before it runs (`403`,
`node::READ_ONLY`), so no run burns the developer's fuel uncounted.
A team's creator leads it.
Below admin, creating an app in the organization needs `caps::CREATE_APP` (on its team, or org-wide)
and minting an invite `caps::INVITE_MEMBERS` (org-wide, or on each team it names); neither is in
`caps::DEFAULT`, and a team's leads hold both there.
`caps::MANAGE_APPLICATION` lets a member upgrade the org's apps (on the app's team, or org-wide),
start org upgrade runs, and approve the org's outbound hosts (org-wide grants only)
(`SystemState::manages_apps`).
A member who left, or whom a manager removed, does not come back by inheritance until a manager adds
them again or an invite names the team (`Team::removed`).
Losing a team takes one's direct rows in the teams below it (`Org::drop_rows_below`), whether by
removal or leaving (`RemoveTeamMember { subtree }`), a `ScopeApps` close, or a plain close
(`UpdateTeam { join: invite }`, for those in it by inheritance alone; `close_drops_below`, which the
API always sets, absent in an entry an earlier release logged, which replays keeping the rows).
Reads are filtered: a team one neither reaches nor manages is not listed (`GET /orgs/{org}`) and
answers `404` (`GET /orgs/{org}/teams/{team}`).
`DeleteTeam` takes the teams below it and their apps.
Metadata (`POST /orgs/{org}/teams/{team}`) is the app's (a folder's name and colour).
A team's `default_capabilities` (`Team`, left out when unset) count for everyone in it without a row
of their own, on top of their org-wide caps (`Org::team_caps`, used by `may` and open-team
inheritance; a new row takes them too); set by `SetDefaultCapabilities { team }` (`POST
/orgs/{org}/capabilities { team, capabilities }`, the team's managers or `MANAGE_MEMBERS`).
In an org with app teams a guest inherits no open top-level team and reaches no app through an `org`
grant (`Org::app_only`, used by `in_team_at` and `may_reach`); a legacy org's guests are as before.

### App teams and modes

Code: `Team::app_package`: `metadata.app.package` on a top-level team; `Org::app_team`, `Org::mode`
= legacy | standalone | shared, derived.

One per package per org (`UpdateTeam` refuses a second, `APP_TEAM_EXISTS`); making, changing or
dropping the mark, scoping apps and creating the first instance of a package with no app team in an
org that has app teams are admin-only (`ONLY_ADMINS_INSTALL`).
`ScopeApps` (`POST /orgs/{org}/app-teams`, admin, passkey) finds or makes each package's team (id
from the entry's `nonce`, `app_team_id`; an instance's package is the one its release names,
`SystemState::instance_package`, since two packages may ship one component, unless the entry
predates `by_release`), gives a row to every member who `may_reach` the instances first, gives them
its `team` grant in place of `org` (`Access::onto_team`), and with `shared` (default: two app teams
or more) closes every app team after writing its inherited people as rows and drops `INVITE_MEMBERS`
from its default (standalone teams it makes default to `INVITE_MEMBERS`); a package's `childTeams`
(`ScopeApp::child_teams`) adds `CREATE_CHILD_TEAM` to (or takes it from) its app team's default and
the rows that set their own capabilities, the only way an app team made before that default gets it;
bounded by `MAX_SCOPE_PACKAGES`, `MAX_SCOPE_INSTANCES`, `MAX_APP_TEAM_NAME_CHARS`.
`GET /orgs/{org}/app-teams` (any member; a guest only theirs).
`GET /orgs/{org}` filters members, capabilities, member metadata and team rows by `Org::sees_member`
(admins all; members all unless shared, then co-team people and admins; guests co-team people).
`SetAccess` is also open to `SystemState::may_set_access` (`MANAGE_APPLICATION` where the app is and
where it goes, org-wide for non-team access).
Invites record `created_by` (set at apply) and `created_at` (both left out when unset); `GET
/orgs/{org}/invites` and `DELETE /orgs/{org}/invites/{key}` (`RevokeInvite`) for whoever
`SystemState::sees_invite`.

### Transfers

`POST /apps/{app}/transfer { to }` (owner, or for an org app its owner while a member or an org
admin, `SystemState::manages_app`; passkey consent; only to a member of the app's org, refused
before the passkey once organizations are ordinary).

### Moves

`POST /apps/{app}/move { toOrg, bringMembers }` (`SystemCmd::MoveApp`, `SystemState::move_app`;
consent `<app> / __move`): the app's manager where it is now, as an admin of `toOrg`.
Data stays put; `AppRecord.org`, the payer's file bytes and compute slots move, within the target's
quotas (`payer_quotas`: its override, else the cluster's, with `MoveApp { org_quotas }`, which the
API always sets; `Feature::MoveOrgQuotas`, rc.24; an entry without it replays judging the cluster's
limits).
`org`/app-team access becomes the target's app team (made open, or closed when every app team of the
target is; `org` access in a legacy target); a team other than the package's app team is refused
(`MOVE_NEEDS_APP_TEAM`), and so is an owner who is not a member of the target afterwards
(`MOVE_OWNER_NOT_IN_TARGET`) and a target that approves none of the releases holding the app
(`MOVE_RELEASES_DISJOINT`); clients see each as `400 REJECTED` with the reason as the message.
`bringMembers` adds the source's members who reach the app, capped at member, with app-team rows.

### Metadata and org lifecycle

(Org/member/app metadata, device labels, owner-delete.)

`Org::metadata` (admins or `MANAGE_METADATA`), `Org::member_metadata` (the member or an admin),
`AppRecord::metadata` (owner, while a member for an org app, or org admin), each at most
`MAX_METADATA_BYTES` (16 KiB, team metadata too); `Account::passkey_labels` (`POST
/auth/passkeys/{cred} { label }`).
`DELETE /orgs/{org}` is the owner's, with a passkey assertion (412 first), and takes the org's
teams, invites and apps.
`POST /orgs/{org}/name` renames it (`RenameOrg`: owner or admin, passkey `__rename_org { name }`,
`system::org_name_fits`, as `CreateOrg { name_fits }` checks a new one).
Ownership moves with `SetOrgRole { role: owner }`; leaving is `RemoveOrgMember` on oneself.

### Organization recovery

Code: `docs/specs/org-recovery.md`; `Org::recovery`, `types::OrgRecovery`.

An org may hold a recovery key (Ed25519 public half; the owner keeps the seed).
`SetRecoveryKey` (owner, consent `__set_recovery_key { publicKey }`; a new key or `null` ends a
pending recovery), `StartRecovery` (any account, with the seed's signature over org, account and
nonce, `calimero_identity::recovery`, checked against the org's current key in `authorize` and
spent), `CancelRecovery` (owner, `__cancel_recovery { account }`), `FinishRecovery` (the named
account, `__finish_recovery { org }`, at least `RECOVERY_DELAY_SECS` (72 h) after the start, judged
at the commands' `at`: the node's clock, `Node::app_clock`, so tests move it).
Finishing removes every previous owner from the org and its teams and moves their org apps to the
new owner; account credentials are never touched.
One pending recovery per org (`409 RECOVERY_PENDING`); `403 WRONG_RECOVERY_KEY` for no org, no key
or a wrong signature alike.
Routes: `POST /orgs/{org}/recovery`, `.../recovery/{start,cancel,finish}`.
